# 80/20 Skill Design

## Purpose

A decision tool for when a solo founder suspects an activity isn't worth their time. Not an audit — a single-activity diagnosis that compares the current approach against higher-leverage alternatives and delivers a kill/refine/pivot recommendation with a concrete action plan.

## Trigger

Founder arrives with a pain statement about a specific activity:
- "I'm spending too much time on X and not getting results"
- "Is X worth my time?"
- "Should I keep doing X or try something else?"
- "I feel stuck on X"

One activity. One decision.

## Core Flow

### Step 1: Understand the activity and its results

Ask 2-3 quick questions to establish the effort-to-result ratio:
- What are you doing?
- How much time per week?
- What results so far?

Not an interrogation. Just enough to diagnose.

### Step 2: Diagnose — wrong activity or wrong execution?

Apply two filters:

**Ceiling test:** Even if you did this perfectly, would it move the needle at your stage? (e.g., cold outreach to enterprise when you have no case studies = low ceiling regardless of execution)

**Execution test:** Are you doing the high-leverage 20% of this activity, or spreading effort across the full 100%? (e.g., sending 100 generic emails vs. 10 deeply personalized ones)

### Step 3: Leverage comparison

Regardless of diagnosis, surface 2-3 alternative activities in the same category (acquisition, retention, revenue, etc.) with:
- Typical effort-to-result ratio at the founder's stage
- Why it might be higher leverage
- What the 80/20 version looks like (the minimum effective dose)

### Step 4: Recommend — kill, refine, or pivot

- **Kill:** Stop entirely. Redirect time to the highest-leverage alternative.
- **Refine:** Keep the activity but cut to the 20% version. Specific instructions on what to cut and what to double down on.
- **Pivot:** The category is right but the tactic is wrong. Switch to a specific alternative.

Each recommendation comes with a concrete action plan: what to do this week, not a strategy document.

## Activity Categories

The skill reasons across the categories a solo founder's time falls into:

- **Customer acquisition** — outreach, content, ads, partnerships, SEO, community, referrals
- **Product** — building features, fixing bugs, design, technical debt
- **Retention** — onboarding, support, feedback loops, engagement
- **Revenue** — pricing, upsells, payment optimization, expansion
- **Operations** — admin, legal, finance, hiring, tools

For each category, the skill carries knowledge of:
- Which tactics are typically highest-leverage at **early stage** (pre-PMF, <$1k MRR) vs. **growth stage** ($1k-$10k MRR) vs. **scaling** ($10k+ MRR)
- What the "minimum effective dose" looks like for each tactic — the 20% version
- Common traps where founders over-invest (e.g., perfecting a blog strategy before having 10 customers)

This isn't a reference table. It's the knowledge Claude uses to make the leverage comparison in Step 3.

## Output Format

Every run ends with a concrete recommendation:

**Verdict:** Kill / Refine / Pivot

**Why:** 2-3 sentences on the diagnosis. What's the effort-to-result ratio, what's the ceiling, how does it compare to alternatives.

**This week:**
- Stop doing [specific thing]
- Start doing [specific thing] — here's the 20% version: [concrete instructions]
- Expected time shift: [X hrs/week freed up → redirected to Y]

**The 80/20 version of [recommended activity]:**
A short, specific description of the minimum effective dose. Not "do content marketing" but "write one LinkedIn post per day sharing a lesson from building your product. No blog, no SEO, no content calendar. Just the post."

**Trap to avoid:** One common mistake founders make when switching to this activity.

## What This Skill Is NOT

- Not a time audit or activity tracker
- Not a comprehensive business review
- Not a strategy document generator
- Not a replacement for prioritize (which scores feature candidates) — this evaluates how you spend your time, not what you build
