# About Me: Founder Profile

Create `ABOUT-ME.md` — a reference file that captures who you are so every other skill can personalize its output. Social media sounds like you. Strategy advice fits your actual situation. Outreach leads with your real credibility.

Without this file, skills produce generic output. With it, they produce output that sounds like a specific human with specific experience, opinions, and a communication style.

## How It Works

1. You write a brain dump about yourself — guided by clear prompts
2. This skill structures your brain dump into a well-organized reference file
3. Other skills read `ABOUT-ME.md` and naturally use it

**The brain dump IS a voice sample.** How you describe yourself reveals your communication style — sentence length, formality, humor, vocabulary. This skill analyzes your writing patterns, not just what you say.

---

## Workflow

### If ABOUT-ME.md already exists

Ask the founder what changed:
- New experience, achievement, or story?
- Communication style evolved?
- Network or distribution changed?
- Strengths or gaps shifted?

Update the relevant sections. Don't start over.

### If creating from scratch

**Step 1 — Present the prompt:**

> Tell me about yourself. Write naturally — like you're explaining to a smart friend who needs to understand who you are in order to write as you and give you advice that fits your actual situation.
>
> Cover whatever feels important, but here are areas that help the most:
> - What you do and how you got here
> - What you know deeply — your unfair expertise
> - Strong opinions you have about your industry
> - A few stories that shaped you — wins, failures, lessons
> - How you communicate (or just write naturally and I'll pick it up)
> - Where you have an existing presence or audience
> - What you're great at vs. what you'd rather not do yourself

Let them write. Don't interrupt. Don't ask follow-up questions unless they missed something critical.

**Step 2 — Process the brain dump:**

Read what they wrote. Organize it into the ABOUT-ME.md sections below. Also analyze their writing itself:
- Sentence length and rhythm
- Vocabulary level (jargon-heavy, plain-spoken, technical-casual)
- Formality (boardroom, coffee shop, text message)
- Humor and personality (dry, self-deprecating, earnest, none)
- Patterns (do they use analogies? stories? data? direct statements?)

Capture this analysis in the Communication Style section. Use their actual writing as evidence — quote specific phrases that illustrate their voice.

**Step 3 — Present for review:**

Show them the structured ABOUT-ME.md. Ask: "Does this sound like you? Anything missing or wrong?"

Revise based on feedback. Write the file when approved.

## ABOUT-ME.md Template

```markdown
# About Me

## Professional Background
[Career arc, current situation, how they got here. Not a resume — the narrative version.]

## Domain Expertise
[What they know deeply. The unfair knowledge advantage. Specific credentials or experience
that give them credibility in their space.]

## Communication Style
[Inferred from how they wrote the brain dump. Includes:
- Natural tone (formal/casual, concise/expansive)
- Vocabulary patterns (plain-spoken, technical, industry jargon)
- Personality markers (humor style, directness, use of stories vs. data)
- Specific phrases or patterns that are distinctly theirs
- What they would NEVER say or write]

## Values & Opinions
[What matters to them. Strong opinions about their industry. Hot takes.
Hills they'd die on. These become the backbone of social content and positioning.]

## Key Stories
[Defining moments, failures, wins, lessons. The anecdotes they retell.
These are raw material for social media, content, and outreach.]

## Network & Distribution
[Where they already have presence — audience size, professional connections,
communities, speaking, publishing. Skills like sales and launch use this
to prioritize warm channels over cold outreach.]

## Strengths & Gaps
[What they're great at. What they need help with. Technical comfort level.
Skills like hiring and prioritize use this to give better advice.]
```

## What Makes a Good ABOUT-ME.md

- **Specific over general.** "15 years running logistics for mid-market e-commerce" beats "extensive business experience."
- **Real opinions, not safe ones.** "I think most project management tools are designed for managers, not the people doing the work" is usable. "I believe in delivering value" is not.
- **Actual stories, not summaries.** "I once lost a $200K client because our onboarding took 3 weeks" gives other skills something to work with. "I've learned from my failures" gives them nothing.
- **Communication Style section should quote the founder.** Not "they're casual" but "they write things like 'Look, here's the deal' and 'This is the part nobody talks about.'"
- **No gaps.** Every section filled. If the founder doesn't have a network yet, say "No existing audience — starting from zero" so skills can adapt.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Writing a LinkedIn bio instead of a brain dump | This isn't a resume. It's a profile for personalizing AI output. Be honest, not polished. |
| Generic opinions ("I believe in quality") | Push for specific, defensible opinions. "Most SaaS onboarding is designed for demos, not real users." |
| Missing the Communication Style section | This is the most important section for copy quality. Always analyze how they wrote, not just what they said. |
| Over-polishing the brain dump before submitting | The raw version captures authentic voice better. Polish removes the signal. |
| Leaving "Strengths & Gaps" vague | Be specific: "I can design in Figma but can't write backend code" beats "I'm a visual thinker." |
